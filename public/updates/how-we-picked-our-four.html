How we picked our four charities

October 2026

We started with four things we want Dia to grow up caring about: families having enough to eat, kids having a safe place to grow, animals finding homes, and the planet they all share.

Then we looked for one charity for each. We wanted a few things to be true:

  • A four-star rating from Charity Navigator.
  • Most of every dollar going to the actual work, ideally 85 cents or more.
  • Not much spent raising money.
  • A long track record of making a real difference.

What surprised us

Some of the best-known names fell short of our bar. Several big national animal and conservation groups spend closer to 70 or 75 cents of each dollar on programs. They still do good work, but we wanted to be strict.

We also learned that smaller local groups with big volunteer programs can look a little less efficient on paper, because the staff who train and support volunteers count as overhead. That's worth knowing if you're comparing charities yourself.

Where we landed

  • Hunger: Alameda County Community Food Bank. 93 cents of each dollar goes to programs.
  • Kids: Boys & Girls Clubs of San Francisco. 86 cents.
  • Animals: Berkeley Humane. 86 cents.
  • Planet: World Wildlife Fund. 79 cents.

Three of the four are close to home, so we can show up in person and not just give online.

The one exception

WWF misses our own 85-cent line. We picked it anyway. Kiran got involved with WWF as a kid, at about Dia's age. And these days Kiran and Dia watch David Attenborough's documentaries together all the time. He's a hero in our house, and two of his series, Our Planet and A Life on Our Planet, were made with WWF. It also has a perfect overall score from Charity Navigator.

If you'd like to give to any of the four, here's where to do it. Every gift goes straight to the charity.
